Visual plan, one line per beat. The first few words of each beat's spoken line are in quotes so they can be used as cues.

"Okay, so you're in a bar"       Noisy waveform, bar ambience, phone mockup.
"So you pull out your phone"     Phone listens, result card pops in (song, artist, album).
"I've done this probably"        StatCaption: tens of millions of songs.
"And it isn't AI doing that"     Paper title card fades in, 2003.
"So my first guess"              Two waveforms sliding over each other (Compare).
"A recording is a wave"          Clean wave gets wrecked as pills stack on: volume, noise, random start.
"Your drink would be warm"       Quick gag beat, a drink icon with a clock.
"So Shazam needed something"     Fingerprint icon. ListCard: tiny, fast to look up, survives noise.
"Any sound you hear"             One wave splits into stacked sine waves (LayerStack).
"Do that for every tiny slice"   Spectrogram builds column by column. Axes: time across, pitch up.
"It only keeps the peaks"        Spectrogram fades to black, bright dots remain.
"looks like a night sky"         Dots twinkle. Label: constellation map.
"And this is why it works"       Noise speckles fill the dark, main stars stay locked in place.
"It connects the dots"           Anchor star with Connectors drawn to a few stars ahead of it.
"Each pair turns into"           KeyValue: pitch 1, pitch 2, gap. Example numbers are made up.
"Shazam did this to every song"  Fingerprints flow into a database, each tagged song + time (Pipeline).
"Then it sends just those"       Phone sends small number chips, not a waveform.
"So you get hits scattered"      Hits pop up across lots of song cards. Chaos.
"Say you started recording"      Clip timeline and song timeline, parallel lines between matches, all offset 62 s.
"For the wrong songs"            Histograms: flat, flat, flat, then one huge spike at 62 with accent glow.
"So, the whole thing"            StepTimeline recap: picture, stars, fingerprints, spike.
"A guy named Avery Wang"         Paper title card, Apple acquisition note (2018).
"It's looking at the stars"      Constellation pulls back into a real night sky. End card.

Fact notes
- Source: Avery Li-Chun Wang, "An Industrial-Strength Audio Search Algorithm", ISMIR 2003. Peaks become a constellation map, anchor/target pairs are hashed as (f1, f2, gap), and matches are found by histogramming time offsets.
- Apple bought Shazam in 2018.
- "Tens of millions of songs" is deliberately loose. Published figures vary.
- The script says "the core idea is still inside Shazam", not that it runs the 2003 code unchanged.
