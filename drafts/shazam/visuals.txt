Visual plan, one line per beat. The first few words of each beat's spoken line are in quotes so they can be used as cues.

"You know when you're somewhere"  Noisy waveform, phone mockup listening, result card appears.
"I've used it for years"          Paper title card, Avery Wang, 2003.
"The first thing you might"       Two waveforms sliding over each other (Compare).
"The problem is that sound"       Clean wave gets messier as "talking" and "random start" pills appear.
"So Shazam needed a kind"         Fingerprint icon.
"The first step is to turn"       One wave splits into stacked sine waves (LayerStack).
"If you do that for every"        Spectrogram builds column by column. Axes: time across, pitch up.
"Next, Shazam throws away"        Spectrogram fades to black, bright dots remain. Label: constellation map.
"The reason this helps"           Noise speckles fill the dark areas, the main dots stay in place.
"A single dot doesn't tell"       Anchor dot with Connectors drawn to a few dots after it.
"For each pair it writes"         KeyValue: pitch 1, pitch 2, time gap. Example numbers are made up.
"Shazam has already done"         Fingerprints flow into a database, each tagged with song and time (Pipeline).
"When you record something"       Phone sends small fingerprint chips to a server.
"The servers then look up"        Matches appear across lots of song cards.
"Say you started recording"       Clip timeline and song timeline, parallel lines between matches, all offset by 62 s.
"The coincidental matches"        Histograms per song: flat for most, one tall spike at 62 s with accent glow.
"So that's the whole process"     StepTimeline recap: picture, peaks, fingerprints, timing.
"That's still the basic idea"     Paper card again, Apple 2018 note, end card.

Fact notes
- Source: Avery Li-Chun Wang, "An Industrial-Strength Audio Search Algorithm", ISMIR 2003. Peaks become a constellation map, anchor/target pairs are hashed as (f1, f2, gap), and matches are found by histogramming time offsets.
- Apple bought Shazam in 2018.
- "Tens of millions of songs" is deliberately loose. Published figures vary.
- The script says "the core idea is still inside Shazam", not that it runs the 2003 code unchanged.
