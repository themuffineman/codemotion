Have you ever wondered how Shazam can name a song from a few seconds of audio in a loud room? Here's how it works.

First it turns the sound into a picture, with time going across and pitch going up. Then it throws most of that picture away and only keeps the loudest points, because background noise usually isn't loud enough to cover them.

It pairs those points up, and each pair, meaning two pitches and the time between them, becomes a fingerprint. Those get looked up against every song Shazam knows.

Lots of songs will match a few fingerprints by coincidence. But in the right song, every match is off by the same amount of time, so when you chart the time differences, that song shows up as one tall spike.

It's based on a paper from 2003, and there's no AI involved. I go through it in more detail in the full video on my channel.
